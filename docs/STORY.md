# SANKOFA LINE: story bible (working title)

*Same ticket. New station.*

Codename in the repo stays "Oni Night". Everything below is **fiction**. Names, companies and people are invented; any resemblance is accidental.
It is inspired by documented patterns, not by any real organisation (see "Ground rules" at the end).

## The one-paragraph pitch

Accra, now, in harmattan season, when the dust turns every sunset orange. Near the University of Ghana, Legon, a company called **Halcyon Passage Group** sells young people a life abroad:
jobs, scholarships, visas, "the Passage". Seminars in hotel ballrooms, friends who suddenly look rich, photos in luxury lobbies. You pay, you go, and the people who go do not come back; they send voice notes that sound a little too calm.
You play **Kwesi Adjei**, a final-year Legon student whose cousin Efua took the Passage. Following her voice notes leads you under Legon hill to a railway that is not on any map: **the Hill Line**,
a colonial spur built in the 1940s, sealed at independence, its records burned. The company has reopened it as a **departure hall**. And something at the end of the line has been waiting a very long time for passengers.

## The idea underneath (why it is huge)

Ghana has lived the same trick three times, with three kinds of ticket:

1. **The Door of No Return.** The Atlantic trade: people sold a different world, then carried off. (Elmina and Cape Coast castles are real places you can visit today.)
2. **The extraction railways.** The colonial lines from the mines and cocoa farms to the coast, built on conscripted labour, carrying out gold, timber and cocoa. The wealth left on trains.
3. **The Passage.** A modern scam that promises a life abroad, takes your money, takes your phone and your papers, and makes you recruit your friends.

In the game these are one thing. **The Stationmaster** is the supernatural thread: a presence that counts people like freight, that has worn many uniforms
(a slaver's clerk, a colonial foreman, a smooth modern recruiter). It does not hate anyone. It only needs the manifest to be *full*. Every Passage that sells a fake future feeds it.

The player's answer to it is **Sankofa**: the Akan idea of going back to fetch what was left behind. You do not win by being stronger. You win by **remembering names and bringing people home**.

## The infected: the Carried

Not bitten, **recruited**. A person who accepts the Offer (a phone call, a pitch, a boarding card called a *Ticket*) slowly empties. They stop sleeping, stop eating, and speak only in the script:
*"Brother, I have an opportunity for you."* They walk the old rails and the streets near them, **bringing others** by the hand, and the more people they bring, the more hollow they become.

- **The Carried**: the basic hollow ones. Slow, patient, not hungry, *insistent*. They grab and lead; you are not bitten, you are walked toward the train.
- **Callers**: the Carried who still have a phone. They lure by voice (a cousin's voice, a friend's), which makes sound and darkness part of the game.
- **Porters**: heavy, silent, carry luggage and people; they block routes and push crowds.
- **The Manifest**: a swarm made of everyone on the night train, moving as one.
- **The Stationmaster**: the boss, in a uniform that keeps changing eras.

**Saving people is the point.** A Carried person can be brought back: find their **name** (an ID card, a voice note, a school photo, a message on a phone) and **say it** when they are close enough.
Each rescue adds a name to the wall of the safehouse. Fighting is possible, but a fight only slows them; a name ends it. This is the emotional core and the reason the game is not just shooting.

## Your powers: the Weight (Adinkra)

The ancestors of the Hill Line (the workers whose names were never written) lend the survivor a **Weight**. It appears as glowing Adinkra symbols on armour made of old Asafo company regalia. (The Asafo were real Fante warrior companies who carried painted flags.)
The repo already has a glowing-circuit armour shader; it becomes the glyph shader.

| Symbol | Meaning | Ability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sankofa** | go back and fetch it | **Echo**: at marked places, see what happened there, as ghost scenes; reveals clues, hidden doors and names |
| **Akoma** | the heart, patience | **Call**: slow a Carried person and let the person inside hear you; the rescue mechanic |
| **Dwennimmen** | ram's horns: strength with humility | **Hold the line**: a shield and charge that grows stronger the more people stand behind you |
| **Nkyinkyim** | twisting: initiative | **Slip**: dodge and parkour through crowds, over walls and across moving trains |
| **Aya** | the fern: endurance | **Endure**: resist cold, dust and exhaustion; long runs; tough when alone |
| **Funtunfunefu-Denkyemfunefu** | the two crocodiles sharing one stomach: unity | **Link**: share health and stamina with an ally; the co-operative power |

Powers unlock with story, not xp: each one is earned by hearing a name and finishing a rescue.

## The cast

- **Kwesi Adjei**: the player. Final-year student, quiet, runs at dawn. His cousin Efua's last voice note is the first thing you hear. (Name and background are changeable.)
- **Auntie Dede**: runs a chop bar near the Night Market. The safehouse, the rumour mill, the cook. Knows every family on campus. Teaches you the names.
- **Opanin Quaye**: retired railway signalman, 80 years old, who worked the last trains on the Accra lines and remembers the Hill Line. Your guide below ground, and slowly you learn why he never left.
- **Dr. Akosua Frimpong**: history lecturer who found a fragment of the burned records. She knows what the Stationmaster is.
- **"Small Boy"**: a trotro mate (the one who shouts the destinations). Knows every route and shortcut. Your vehicle and your humour.
- **Efua**: your cousin. Alive? You find out in act three.
- **"Coach Delali"**: the smiling face of Halcyon, a recruiter who sells the Passage. Not a monster; a salesman who has stopped asking what he sells.
- **The Chairman**: never seen. Only a voice on a phone and a signature on a ticket.
- **The Stationmaster**: see above.

## Structure

**Prologue: Efua's last voice note.** Evening on the Balme Library steps. Walk, listen, decide to follow her.

**Act 1: Campus, night.** The Night Market, a hall quad, the bush canteen, the library, the botanical gardens. The first Carried, the first rescue, and the first Echo.
Dumsor (the power cuts) turns campus dark in waves and the Carried move in the dark. Generators are lifelines, and also noise.

**Act 2: The roads.** Madina, Okponglo, Haatso, East Legon: markets, trotro stations, half-built compounds. Rescue crowds, run a trotro through traffic, reach the old rail yard.
The imagined railway appears: a **night train** that stops at a halt that is not on any map.

**Act 3: Under the hill.** The Hill Line depot, the tunnels, the departure hall. Echoes show the line's history in layers (1940s workers, a 1957 night, a modern seminar).
Efua is found, and you decide what saves her and what it costs.

**Finale: The last train.** A fight on a moving train, with the Manifest, Coach Delali's last offer and the Stationmaster. You win by calling every name you have gathered.

## Set-pieces (the "wow" list)

- Running across the roofs of a moving train at dusk with the Manifest pouring out of the doors.
- A trotro chase on the Madina road, with the mate hanging out of the door shouting destinations.
- A **dumsor** blackout sweeping the campus block by block, and you racing to reach a generator.
- A **harmattan** dust-out where visibility drops to 20 m and the Carried hum the same jingle.
- A hotel ballroom seminar frozen in time: rows of chairs, a projector, a pitch deck that still plays.
- An unfinished concrete building where the Carried stand on every floor, facing the road.
- The Echo of 1957 on the platform: a night the Stationmaster burned the books.
- The rescue wall: a long tiled wall in the safehouse where each name appears as you save someone.

## Imagined additions to the real map

Things that do not exist in Legon (our own touch):

- **The Hill Line**: sealed colonial spur from the Accra rail corridor to a depot under Legon hill, with a ruined halt (platform, water tower, signal box).
- **The Night Train**: a ghost train that runs only during dumsor and carries passengers to the departure hall.
- **A suspended tram** around campus (the Passage's marketing promised it as "the future"; half-built and abandoned, its pylons now part of the skyline).
- **Halcyon Tower**: a glass sales office at the edge of East Legon with a seminar floor and a basement door.
- **The Atomic gap**: the quiet corridor between the university and the real atomic energy site where the Line crosses (the site is real; its use in the story is invented and never shown to harm real people).

## Ground rules (so we can ship it)

- **No real company is named as a villain.** The real cases are documented by police and Interpol: people lured with fake jobs and visa offers, taken to other countries, held, and forced to recruit others.
  Authorities have linked some cases to multi-level-marketing networks; companies named in the press dispute involvement and say criminals impersonate them.
  Putting a real firm in a game as the kidnapper would be inaccurate and legally risky, so Halcyon is invented and its tactics are the documented *pattern*.
- **No real victims, no real people.** No real names, faces or cases. No real students or lecturers.
- **The university is not the villain.** Campus is the place the story happens, and the story never suggests that the real institution is complicit. A clear fiction notice goes in the credits.
- **Trafficking is treated seriously.** The Carried are a metaphor for the scam, not for real victims. No gore aimed at the victim; the horror is the emptiness, and the goal is rescue.
- **Credits include a real support line** for people who think they or someone they know is being recruited (Ghana's anti-human-trafficking resources). We will use a verified number before launch.
- **History is told with care.** Echoes come from documented history (the Atlantic trade, colonial railway labour, 1948 and 1957), checked with a Ghanaian historian before release.
- Adinkra and Asafo imagery are used respectfully, with credit and (ideally) review by Akan cultural advisers.
